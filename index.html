What I build

Three kinds of projects, often for the same business: the site customers see, and the tools your team uses to keep up with them.

01 · For your customers

Websites that bring in business

A fast, good-looking site with the parts that turn visitors into customers: book an appointment, request a quote, see the work.

  • Design and build
  • Booking and quote forms
  • Photo galleries
  • Search basics and analytics

02 · For your team

Custom tools to run the business

Admin panels and dashboards that replace spreadsheets and sticky notes, built around how your shop actually works.

  • Customer and job tracking
  • Quotes and printable paperwork
  • Inventory and QR codes
  • Connections to Trello, email and more

03 · For a bigger idea

Web apps, start to finish

For a new product or an idea that needs more than a website: accounts, data and the interface, built and deployed for you.

  • Accounts and permissions
  • Databases and APIs
  • Maps, 3D and interactive screens
  • Hosting and ongoing support

How it works

No jargon, no handoffs. You talk to the person writing the code from start to finish.

  1. 01

    Talk

    Tell me what's slowing the business down, or what you want customers to be able to do.

  2. 02

    Plan

    You get a written proposal: what I'll build, how long it takes and what it costs.

  3. 03

    Build

    Regular updates and a working preview you can click through as it comes together.

  4. 04

    Launch

    We go live, I show your team how it works, and I stay on for fixes and changes.

Hi, I'm Andrew

My full name is Andrew Dong Kyu Kim. I'm Korean, born in Buffalo, and I've been building for the web since 2017: five years on logistics software at NTG, apps for startups as a contractor, and now websites and tools for small businesses.

Before code, I was a b-boy. Breaking taught me what I still bring to every project: practice until the hard part looks easy, and show up for your crew. I'm also a lifelong Knicks and Bills fan, so I know a thing or two about patience.

The name ADK is my initials. The K in the logo is half of a star, because my Korean name, 東奎, means east star.
